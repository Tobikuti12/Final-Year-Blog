NeuroFlex360

Final Year Project | BSc IT - Middlesex University Dubai

Hey! I'm a final year student developing a digital solution for cognitive rehabilitation, specifically designed for elderly and stroke patients.

About the Project

NeuroFlex360 is a mobile application focused on brain exercises. It's built to be scalable and accessible globally without needing expensive physical equipment, making rehabilitation easier for patients everywhere.

Weekly Progress

Week 1: Brainstorming & First Blog

October 2025

Started the semester by exploring gaps in the healthcare tech market. Researched how digital tools can assist in long-term cognitive recovery. Published my first blog post introducing the project concept and the anatomy of a dissertation.

Week 2: Final Proposal & Introduction Chapter

October 2025

Finalised the project proposal with a clear project plan, Gantt chart, and deliverables. Completed the MDX ethics form covering participant anonymity and data privacy. Also drafted the Introduction chapter of my dissertation covering the background, problem statement, aim, and scope.

Week 3: Literature Review Begins

November 2025

Explored different research methodologies — qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods. Selected 5–7 peer-reviewed articles on cognitive rehabilitation and digital health tools. Started drafting Chapter 2 of the dissertation, grouping sources by theme and identifying gaps in existing research.

Week 4: Literature Review Completed

November 2025

Completed Chapter 2 of the dissertation. Synthesised all reviewed literature, highlighted key findings, and identified the main gap NeuroFlex360 aims to address: the lack of accessible, clinician-monitored digital rehab tools for elderly users. Submitted the final literature review draft.

Week 5: Software Methodology & Prototyping

December 2025

Compared Waterfall, Agile, Scrum, and Spiral methodologies. Chose Agile for its flexibility and iterative cycles — ideal for a user-facing health app. Built the first prototype wireframes focusing on core functionalities, then tested them to gather initial feedback for refinement.

Week 6–7: UML Diagrams & Prototype Evaluation

January 2026

Completed all four UML diagrams: use case, class, sequence, and activity diagrams. Conducted user testing on the prototype, documented feedback, and refined the design. Also formalised the Software Requirements Specification covering functional and non-functional requirements and test plans.

Week 8: Implementation & Development

February 2026

Set up the full development environment and built the core features of NeuroFlex360 — all three cognitive modules (Memory, Attention, Reaction Time), Firebase authentication, Firestore score storage, and the Node.js backend deployed on Render. Conducted unit and integration testing throughout.

Week 9: Software Testing

March 2026

Studied and applied different types of software testing: unit, integration, system, and acceptance testing. Wrote and executed formal test cases for all major features. Documented results with expected vs. actual outcomes and fixed all critical bugs identified during the process.

Week 10: Conclusion Chapter

April 2026

Completed the final chapter of the dissertation. Summarised overall project outcomes, evaluated the effectiveness of each module, and reflected on skills gained throughout the year. Identified key limitations and proposed future improvements such as AI-based difficulty adaptation.

Week 11: Software Patents & Intellectual Property

April 2026

Attended a lecture by Dr. Chinnu Mary George on Understanding Software Patents and Intellectual Property. Covered copyright, trade secrets, patents, and trademarks as they apply to software. Key takeaway: NeuroFlex360's source code is automatically protected by copyright, and the adaptive difficulty algorithm could potentially qualify as a patentable process.
